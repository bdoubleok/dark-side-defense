# 📖 Reference: Seattle’s Checkdown Loop — #1 EPA Blitz System

**Source:** [MatchQuarters by Cody Alexander](https://www.matchquarters.com/p/seahawks-checkdown-loop-efficient-blitz-system)
**Published:** April 27, 2026
**Context:** 2025 Seattle Seahawks — Super Bowl Champions, 6th-best defense of all time (FTN DVOA)

-----

## Core Concept: Pressure Without a Premium Pass-Rusher

Macdonald built the NFL’s most *efficient* blitz system by blitzing rarely and surgically — not constantly. The system is built on **coverage constraints doing the real work**, with pressure as the change-up that creates hesitation.

-----

## Key Principles

### 1. Quality Over Quantity

- Finished **25th in Blitz Rate** — barely blitzed
- Blitzes appeared in **top 10 only on 3rd & Long (10+ yards)**
- Of 48 sacks, **20 came via blitz** (6th in NFL)
- **23% sack conversion rate** when blitzing (8th best)

### 2. Stunt-to-Blitz Duality

- Stunted (moved D-line) on **39% of passing downs** (2nd in NFL)
- When blitzing, **stunt rate dropped to only 26%** (25th)
- Dropping stunts on blitz downs kept pressure paths vertical and uncluttered
- Result: **unblocked “quick” pressures under 2.5 seconds**, 17% higher rate than non-blitz snaps
- Only the Houston Texans held a better Pressure Rate than Seattle when blitzing

### 3. The Open Rate Paradox

- Seattle allowed the **highest receiver open rate at 47.2%** when blitzing — intentionally
- Receivers were “open” because coverage funneled the ball exactly where Seattle wanted:
  - Short of the sticks
  - Low in the zone
  - In front of the secondary
- **Opposing QB results when blitzed:**
  - 2nd-lowest YPA: **6.2 yards**
  - Averaged **-1.07 yards short of the sticks** (ALEX)
  - Held **~4 yards per drop-back (YPD)** regardless of coverage called

### 4. The Checkdown Loop

- Disguised coverage rotations created QB hesitation pre-throw
- Seattle finished **3rd in checkdown rate (~14%)** — near league-high
- The loop: pressure → hesitation → checkdown → limited gain → repeat
- Every blitz was married to a coverage concept that funneled the ball somewhere predictable

### 5. Modular Pressure Design

- Pared-down blitz package — not a volume approach
- Built around three core pressure concepts:
  - **Sand** — edge pressure with Cover 2 behind it (Sand Deuce)
  - **Nail** — interior manipulation, protection overload
  - **Pick** — route to a free hitter via protection rules
- Leveraged **alignment and protection manipulation** over spamming paths
- Blitzing was a “highly targeted change-up” to the structural base

## CFB ’26 Takeaway

> **Blitzing is a surgical change-up, not a base call. Coverage constraints do the real work.**

- Default to **sim pressures and stunts** — not true blitzes
- Reserve true blitzes for **3rd & Long (10+)** situations
- When blitzing, **drop stunt complexity** — keep pressure paths clean and vertical
- Pair every blitz with a **coverage concept that funnels the ball short**
- The goal isn’t a sack — it’s **creating hesitation that feeds the checkdown loop**

This confirms the core principle of the Dark Side build:
**Space control first → pressure second → checkdown trap always.**
